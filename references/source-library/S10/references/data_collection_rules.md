# Data Collection Rules

Use this reference when reviewing sampling, sample size, measurement, reliability, validity, conceptualization, operationalization, and measurement scales. These rules come from `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf` and extend the existing research design rules.

## Scientific Sampling

- [course norm] Define the population, sample, sampling process, and unit of analysis before making claims from data. The unit of analysis may be a person, voter, household, news item, document, event, or other research object. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.1.
- [course norm] Sampling is used when the population is infinite, too large, costly, destructive to examine fully, or when sampling can produce quicker or more accurate results. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.1.
- [course norm] Sample size depends on method, funding, inference goals, number of variables, expected attrition, and relevant prior studies. Small samples require especially cautious interpretation and inference. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.2.
- [course norm] Larger samples are generally preferable, but an inappropriate sampling method can make a large sample less representative than a smaller well-sampled one; sampling method matters more than sample size alone. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.2.
- [course norm] For random sampling in social science research, when sampling error is 5% and confidence level is 95%, the minimum sample size is commonly about 384; 400-500 is often used. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.2.
- [course norm] Probability sampling gives each unit in the population an equal chance of selection; examples include simple random sampling, systematic sampling, stratified sampling, and cluster sampling. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.2-p.3.
- [course norm] Systematic sampling must avoid periodicity and ordering bias; shuffle ordering where needed. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.2.
- [course norm] Stratification should use variables tied to major research variables, strong within-layer homogeneity, between-layer heterogeneity, or clear existing strata; proportional and disproportional stratification have different uses. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.3.
- [course norm] Cluster sampling is simpler and cheaper and does not require a full element list, but sample distribution is narrower and representativeness is relatively weaker. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.3.
- [course norm] Non-probability sampling is based on experience or conditions rather than equal probability; its major limitation is that sampling error cannot be judged and the sample cannot be used to infer the population. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.3.
- [course norm] Purposive sampling samples a specific group by predefined criteria; its key issue is the justification of selection criteria, and it cannot support population inference. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.3.
- [course norm] Quota and purposive sampling are relatively better among non-probability approaches; accidental sampling is especially prone to sampling error; volunteer sampling is generally considered valueless or misleading. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.4.

## Precise Measurement

- [course norm] Reliability means measurement stability and consistency. External reliability concerns consistency over time; internal reliability matters when multiple items measure one variable. Common methods include retest, parallel forms, split-half, and Cronbach's alpha. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.4.
- [course norm] Validity means the instrument measures the intended concept rather than another concept, and measures it accurately. Forms mentioned include face/content/logical validity, content validity, and predictive validity. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.5.
- [course norm] Reliability is a necessary condition for validity but not a sufficient condition. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.5.
- [course norm] Conceptualization is defining the concrete meaning of a research concept; a working definition should fit the research purpose and context. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.5.
- [course norm] Operationalization translates abstract concepts into measurable indicators. Options include directly adopting mature scales, adapting scales, or developing new scales; developing new scales is not recommended for undergraduate and master's papers. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.5.
- [course norm] Choose measurement scales appropriate to the variable: nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio. Source: `第七讲 研究数据的搜集.pdf`, p.6.

## Pass / Partial / Fail Criteria

| Criterion | Pass | Partial | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population/sample/unit | Population, sample, sampling process, and unit of analysis are explicit | Some elements are named but boundaries are unclear | Any core element is missing |
| Sampling method | Method fits inference goal and limitations are stated | Method named but rationale or limits thin | Method absent or incompatible with claims |
| Sample size | Size is justified by method, variables, attrition, prior studies, or inference goal | Size is stated but weakly justified | Size missing or used to overclaim |
| Probability vs non-probability | Inference claims match sampling type | Some caution present but too weak | Non-probability sample used for population inference |
| Reliability | Stability/consistency is discussed when measurement uses scales/items | Reliability mentioned but not linked to instrument | Measurement scale used with no reliability concern |
| Validity | Indicators measure intended concepts and coverage is justified | Some concept-indicator fit issues | Indicators measure another concept or concept is undefined |
| Operationalization | Concept-to-indicator translation is clear and suitable | Operationalization exists but needs adjustment | Abstract concept has no measurable indicator |
| Measurement scale | Nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio level matches analysis | Level implied but not explicit | Analysis assumes a scale the data cannot support |

## Conflict / Applicability Notes

- This file does not override the prior rule that method must fit the research question. It adds detail for sampling and measurement.
- The "400-500" sample guideline applies to random sampling under the stated 5% sampling error and 95% confidence conditions; it should not be imposed on qualitative interviews, focus groups, textual analysis, or purposive case studies.
- Non-probability samples can be useful for exploring specific groups or mechanisms, but should not be used to infer population-level prevalence or causal effects.
