# Research Design Rubric

Use this when evaluating methods, data, variables, cases, sampling, measurement, operationalization, analysis logic, ethics, and causal claims. For detailed sampling and measurement rules, also load `data_collection_rules.md`. For quantitative cleaning, coding, descriptive statistics, test selection, and regression strategy, also load `data_analysis_rules.md`.

## Core Function

- [course norm] Research design is the strategy or path used to answer a research question or test a hypothesis.
- [course norm] Design must specify the problem/hypothesis, method and materials, and concrete research plan.
- [course norm] Method choice should follow the research question rather than model novelty.
- [course norm] Empirical design should define population, sample, sampling process, and unit of analysis when making data-based claims.
- [course norm] Measurement should address conceptualization, operationalization, reliability, validity, and appropriate measurement scale.

## Pass / Partial / Fail Criteria

| Criterion | Pass | Partial | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question-design fit | Method clearly answers the question | Fit is plausible but under-explained | Method cannot answer the question |
| Data/material traceability | Source, generation, scope, period, and boundary are clear | Some details missing | Data/materials are vague or invented-looking |
| Sampling/case logic | Sample/cases and selection logic are explicit | Sample exists but rationale unclear | Sample size, process, or boundary absent |
| Operationalization | Indicators match concepts and have variation | Some concept-indicator mismatch | Indicators do not measure concepts |
| Reliability/validity | Reliability and validity are addressed for scales, indicators, or coding | Mentioned but not connected to instrument | Measurement instrument is unchecked |
| Measurement scale | Nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio level fits analysis | Scale level implied but not explicit | Analysis exceeds what the scale supports |
| Analysis procedure | Procedure connects evidence to claims | Procedure named but thin | Method is named only abstractly |
| Statistical fit | Statistical test/model fits variable levels and assumptions | Test/model plausible but assumptions not checked | Test/model incompatible with data |
| Data analysis preparation | Cleaning, coding, missing values, and transformations are documented | Some preparation steps missing | Raw data handling is not explained |
| Causal discipline | Claim strength matches design | Some causal language needs softening | Design cannot support stated causality |
| Limits/ethics | Limits and ethical risks are specific | Generic limits only | Limits/ethics absent despite relevance |

## Method Fit Reminders

- [course norm] Questionnaire studies support large samples and multivariable analysis, but causal order is hard to determine.
- [course norm] Controlled experiments are strong for causal inference but have artificiality and external validity limits.
- [course norm] Content analysis is non-interventionist and economical but may only reveal the "front stage" and often needs other methods.
- [course norm] Interviews can produce rich material but depend heavily on respondent differences and researcher skill.
- [course norm] Observation can produce first-hand material but has intervention and ethics risks.
- [course norm] Small samples require cautious interpretation; non-probability samples cannot support population inference.
- [course norm] Sampling method is more important than sample size alone; a large poorly sampled dataset may be less representative than a smaller well-sampled one.
- [general academic writing suggestion] Mixed methods need an integration logic, not merely two methods placed side by side.

## Workflow

1. Restate the claim the design must support.
2. Identify method type: quantitative, qualitative, mixed, comparative, historical, content analysis, interview, observation, experiment, survey, or other.
3. Extract data/material details: source, time, place, population, sample/corpus, unit of analysis, selection, collection, cleaning/coding.
4. Extract operationalization: dependent variable, independent variable, controls, concepts, working definitions, indicators, categories, coding rules, measurement scales.
5. Check sampling logic, reliability, validity, and whether indicators measure the intended concepts.
6. Compare claim strength with design strength.
7. Mark risks:
   - `Method Mismatch`
   - `Operationalization Gap`
   - `Sampling Gap`
   - `Data Provenance Gap`
   - `Measurement Reliability Gap`
   - `Measurement Validity Gap`
   - `Data Cleaning Gap`
   - `Statistical Test Mismatch`
   - `Regression Strategy Gap`
   - `Evidence Gap`
   - `Causal Overclaim`
8. Propose safe wording and missing information requests.
