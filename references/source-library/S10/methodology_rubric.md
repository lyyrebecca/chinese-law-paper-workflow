# Methodology Rubric

## Required Elements

- Research design: qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods, comparative, historical, ethnographic, survey, interview, content analysis, experiment, or theory-building.
- Case selection: why these cases, sites, texts, groups, periods, or observations?
- Data: what sources are used, how were they collected, and what are their limits?
- Operationalization: how are concepts turned into variables, indicators, codes, themes, or observations?
- Analysis: what procedure connects evidence to claims?
- Validity: what threats, biases, confounders, or interpretive limits remain?

## Claim Discipline

- Causal claims require design logic that addresses alternative explanations.
- Comparative claims require comparable units and clear comparison criteria.
- Interpretive claims require transparent textual, historical, or ethnographic grounding.
- Descriptive claims require reliable sources and clear scope conditions.
- Normative claims require explicit values or criteria of evaluation.

## Revision Questions

- Could another researcher understand what was done?
- Are the data appropriate for the question?
- Are concepts measured or interpreted consistently?
- Are limitations named without weakening the whole paper unnecessarily?
