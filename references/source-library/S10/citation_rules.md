# Citation Rules

## Source Use

- Cite scholarly claims, empirical facts, theories, definitions, statistics, and paraphrased ideas.
- Place citations near the claim they support.
- Use primary sources for data, laws, policy documents, speeches, and original statements when possible.
- Use peer-reviewed or field-recognized sources for theoretical and empirical claims.
- Do not cite a source for a claim it does not actually make.

## Integration

- Introduce a source by explaining its role in the argument.
- Prefer paraphrase for general ideas and reserve quotation for distinctive wording or primary evidence.
- After quoting, interpret the quote and connect it to the paper's claim.
- Avoid citation dumping at the end of broad paragraphs.

## Hygiene

- Keep one citation style consistent throughout.
- Verify author names, years, titles, journal names, volume/issue, pages, DOI, and URLs.
- Mark uncertain citations rather than guessing.
- Use citation managers or library metadata when available.
