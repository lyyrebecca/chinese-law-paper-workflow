# Paper Structure

## Standard Structure

1. Title: name the topic and, when possible, the relationship or puzzle.
2. Introduction: research question, empirical or theoretical puzzle, stakes, thesis, contribution, roadmap.
3. Literature review: organize prior work into debates and show the gap or tension.
4. Theory or framework: define concepts and explain expected relationships or interpretive lens.
5. Methodology: explain design, case selection, data, measurement, analysis, and limitations.
6. Analysis: present evidence in an order that tests or develops the argument.
7. Discussion: interpret findings, address alternatives, and connect back to theory.
8. Conclusion: restate answer, contribution, limitations, and future research.

## Introduction Formula

- Context: what social or scholarly phenomenon is being examined?
- Puzzle: what is surprising, contested, or insufficiently explained?
- Question: what exactly does the paper ask?
- Argument: what answer does the paper defend?
- Contribution: what does this add to a debate, case, concept, or method?
- Roadmap: how will the paper proceed?

## Paragraph Test

Each body paragraph should have:

- A topic sentence with an analytic claim.
- Evidence or reasoning that supports the claim.
- Interpretation that explains why the evidence matters.
- A link back to the thesis or next step in the argument.
