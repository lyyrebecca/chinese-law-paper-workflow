# Literature Review Rubric

## Strong Literature Reviews

- Group sources by scholarly conversation, not by reading order.
- Identify consensus, disagreement, missing mechanisms, neglected cases, or methodological limits.
- Explain how each cluster of literature shapes the paper's question.
- End with a gap or tension that the paper can actually address.

## Common Structures

- Debate structure: school A argues X, school B argues Y, this paper resolves or reframes the tension.
- Mechanism structure: prior work identifies mechanisms A, B, and C; this paper tests or extends one mechanism.
- Case structure: the literature explains similar cases, but this case reveals an overlooked condition.
- Method structure: existing work relies on one method; this paper adds evidence through another design.
- Concept structure: scholars use a concept inconsistently; this paper clarifies and applies it.

## Diagnostic Questions

- What conversation is the paper entering?
- What does each literature cluster explain well?
- What does each cluster miss?
- How does the research question emerge from that gap?
- Does the thesis answer a problem the literature review has made visible?
